# wellmodel

An open, scriptable oil-well modelling toolkit in Python, built around five things commercial nodal tools do poorly or not at all:

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Test data QC** | Flags each well test pass / warn / fail with a plain-language reason: range checks, GOR vs Rsb, a physical VLP envelope, implied-PI outliers against a robust time trend, test stability, water-cut jumps. Weights feed straight into the match. |
| **Automated multi-test matching** | Fits gravity multiplier, friction multiplier and PI to *all* good tests at once (robust least squares on gauge-pressure and rate residuals), with standard errors, a parameter correlation matrix and warnings for out-of-range or unidentifiable multipliers. |
| **Correlation selection** | Ranks VLP correlations by leave-one-out cross-validation - each test predicted by a model that never saw it - and ranks any correlation that needs out-of-range multipliers behind those that don't. Writes a rationale. |
| **Probabilistic runs** | Latin-hypercube Monte Carlo over any inputs, with match covariance propagating model-parameter uncertainty jointly. P90/P50/P10, probability of natural flow, Spearman sensitivities and a tornado. |
| **Transient behaviour** | Pressure-transient solution with wellbore storage, skin and a closed boundary (Stehfest); Bourdet derivative; build-up fitting for k, skin, C and an independent PI. Depletion forecast with material balance + aquifer, coupled to nodal at every step, reporting when the well drops below Pb, goes unstable and stops flowing. |

Everything is vectorised: thousands of nodal solutions (Monte Carlo samples, test points, rate grids) run through the tubing in one pass.

## Physics

- **PVT (black oil):** Standing Pb/Rs/Bo (optionally calibrated to a measured Pb), Vasquez-Beggs undersaturated Bo and viscosity, Beggs-Robinson viscosity, Dranchuk-Abou-Kassem Z, Lee-Gonzalez-Eakin gas viscosity, McCain water, Baker-Swerdloff surface tension.
- **IPR:** composite straight-line PI / Vogel (liquid basis); Darcy PSS PI helper.
- **VLP:** Beggs & Brill (Payne-corrected), modified Hagedorn & Brown (Griffith bubble flow, no-slip floor), Gray (gas wells), Mukherjee & Brill (uphill), Orkiszewski (Griffith bubble, Griffith-Wallis slug, Duns & Ros mist/transition; no-slip density floor), Aziz-Govier-Fogarasi (with Duns & Ros mist), Beggs & Brill no-slip, no-slip homogeneous (lower bound). Duns & Ros (original: F1-F7, L1/L2 and f2 charts digitised from Brill & Beggs 1991, Figs 3-11 to 3-14; Duns & Ros mist with the d - eps correction and the modified gas density in transition) and Fancher & Brown (1963: no-slip density with the energy-loss factor digitised from Figs 7-9 for the three GLR ranges). Fancher & Brown and no-slip act as lower bounds and Duns & Ros as the slug-flow upper bound in the comparison's data checks. Duns & Ros (modified): Duns & Ros holdup on the Gould, Tek & Katz (1974) flow-regime map - Wallis bubble boundary, holdup interpolated across the bubble/slug transition band, plug region (1-in. and 3.15-in. curves, interpolated by tubing size) treated as slug - with Kleyweg friction in bubble and slug flow: a single-phase Moody friction factor on the average (mixture) velocity with holdup-weighted density and viscosity. The froth region has no drawn boundary in the paper and is treated as slug flow; mist flow keeps the Duns & Ros gas-film friction. The exact mixture properties in Kleyweg's method are an interpretation, so results may differ somewhat from commercial implementations. Ansari et al. (1994) mechanistic model: Taitel-Dukler bubble/slug and Barnea dispersed-bubble (void < 0.76) and annular-stability transitions; drift-flux bubble flow, no-slip dispersed bubble, Fernandes/Sylvester slug unit (Taylor bubble, Brotz falling film, slug-body balance) and Alves et al. annular film/core model with Wallis entrainment, its film thickness solved from the combined momentum balance. Not available: Petroleum Experts 1-5 (proprietary, not planned). Digitised chart values carry a reading uncertainty of a few per cent. Orkiszewski and Aziz switch regimes abruptly, as published; Aziz in particular can misbehave at high liquid rates where its map gives mist flow. Deviation survey, tapered strings, linear temperature profile. Heun-integrated top-down traverse.
- **Nodal:** stable (right-hand) IPR/VLP intersection with stability flag at the VLP minimum.

Oilfield units throughout: psia, °F, STB/d, scf/STB, ft, in, cp.

## Quick start

```bash
pip install -e ".[app]"
streamlit run app.py            # point-and-click app in your browser
pytest -q                       # 105 tests
jupyter notebook notebooks/demo.ipynb
```

### The Streamlit app (`app.py`)

The app is being rebuilt tab by tab. It currently has six tabs:

1. **Options summary** - fluid type, method, flow type, well type, completion and sand control. Checks the combination, shows what the calculation engine can model today, and lists the data the later setup tabs will need.
2. **PVT Input Data** - shows black-oil or compositional input depending on the Method chosen in tab 1. **Dry and wet gas (black oil)**, as PROSPER's gas input screen: separator gas gravity, separator pressure, CGR, condensate gravity, WGR, salinity, H2S/CO2/N2 and the gas viscosity correlation (Lee et al or Carr et al); the condensate is carried in the well as an equivalent gas (wellstream gravity recombined from the separator gas and condensate). Reservoir pressure and temperature give the **minimum WGR** when *Calculate condensed water vapour* is chosen in the Options summary (McKetta-Wehe water content with a salinity correction): the gas leaves the reservoir saturated, and in the well it holds the saturated content at local conditions - up to all the water produced - so free water appears where the gas can no longer hold it; the VLP carries the vapour with the gas. A Calculate table (gas density, viscosity, FVF, Z, water properties, pseudo-pressure, free water) at chosen temperatures and pressures, with plots and CSV. Against PROSPER's Tutorial 02 tables (400 points, 60-250 °F, 15-5300 psig): Z, gas FVF and density within 0.8 % (mean -0.1 %), Lee viscosity within 0.9 % (0.01 % at PROSPER's own density), Carr viscosity within 5 % (PROSPER reads the Carr chart; Dempsey's fit here), pseudo-pressure within 2 % above 300 psig (PROSPER integrates gauge pressure, as here), free-water onset at 397 of 400 points; minimum WGR 0.2853 against 0.27114 STB/MMscf (+5 %: PROSPER's water content is a 'modified' McKetta-Wehe).

   **Equation of state:** import a Petroleum Experts PRP export (.prp). The tool reads the EOS (Peng-Robinson or SRK), components, binary interaction coefficients, volume shifts, separator train and reference temperature, then calculates the saturation pressure (bubble or dew), separator-train GOR/CGR, stock-tank API and gas gravity, Bo or Bg, phase properties versus pressure (LBC viscosity, parachor IFT, liquid dropout) and the phase envelope. It warns when the fluid's behaviour contradicts the fluid type chosen in tab 1.

   **Black oil:** as in PROSPER, matching is optional. The selected correlations (bubble point / GOR / FVF family and viscosity) can be chosen at any time and are used as published until a match is made and *Apply match parameters* is ticked; *Reset match* returns every tab to the unmatched correlations. Inputs: solution GOR, oil and gas gravity, water salinity and H2S/CO2/N2, plus an optional lab-data table (pressure, GOR, oil FVF, oil viscosity) with temperature and bubble point. **Match** fits Parameter 1 (multiplier) and Parameter 2 (shift) for every correlation, reports the standard deviation, and suggests the Pb/Rs/Bo family and viscosity correlation needing the smallest correction. Shows match plots and a calculated PVT table.

   - Bubble point / GOR / FVF: Glaso, Standing, Lasater, Vasquez-Beggs, Petrosky et al, Al-Marhoun.
   - Viscosity: Beal et al, Beggs et al, Petrosky et al, Egbogah et al.
   - De Ghetto et al, Bergman-Sutton and De Ghetto Mod are listed but not calculated: their coefficients could not be verified against a published source (De Ghetto Mod is PROSPER's own unpublished variant).

4. **Equipment Data** - five sub-tabs, with a status table showing which sections are complete:

   - **Deviation survey:** up to 20 MD/TVD points, with calculated angle and cumulative displacement and a well-path plot. **Filter a longer survey** accepts any number of points (paste, or upload CSV/TXT/Excel) and keeps the 20 that best reproduce the TVD profile, reporting the largest and RMS TVD error.
   - **Surface equipment:** manifold, line pipe, choke and fittings (equivalent length from L/D values), surrounding temperature and overall heat transfer coefficient. As in PROSPER, each type opens only its own cells (manifold: TVD; line pipe: length, TVD, ID, roughness, rate multiplier; choke: ID, rate multiplier; fitting: fitting type, ID, rate multiplier), with roughness 0.0006 in and rate multiplier 1 as editable defaults. Locked cells are shaded in the table and take no input.
   - **Downhole equipment:** Xmas tree, tubing, SSSV, restriction, gas lift mandrel, SSD and casing, PROSPER-style. As in PROSPER, each type opens only its own cells (Xmas tree: MD; tubing: MD, ID, roughness, rate multiplier; SSSV: ID; restriction, gas lift mandrel and SSD: ID, rate multiplier - placed at the previous depth and treated as restrictions in the pressure calculation; casing: MD, ID, roughness, rate multiplier; tubing rows also take tubing OD/outside roughness and casing ID/roughness - required for annular flow, used by gas lift) - anything typed in a locked cell is cleared, and roughness (0.0006 in) and rate multiplier (1) fill in as editable defaults. Locked cells are shaded in the table itself and refuse input (AG Grid, via `streamlit-aggrid`); without that package the app falls back to a plain table with a shaded guide. Checks depths, diameters and roughness, requires tubing OD and casing ID when annular flow is selected, and checks the string against the survey. Shows flow sections and components.
   - **Geothermal gradient:** formation temperature against MD (TVD from the survey), gradient per interval and the overall heat transfer coefficient.
   - **Average heat capacities:** oil 0.53, gas 0.51, water 1.0 BTU/lb/F by default.
   - **Well schematic (sidebar):** appears in the sidebar once every section is complete - an illustrated, not-to-scale drawing of the Xmas tree, flowline and choke, casing, tubing and its components (SSSV with control line, gas lift mandrels, SSDs, restrictions), tubing end and reservoir, showing perforations or open hole and the sand control from tab 1. The Equipment tab shows the string summary (MD, ID, TVD, formation temperature); the sidebar has an SVG download.

5. **VLP** - well test table laid out like PROSPER's (oil: THP, THT, water cut, liquid rate, gauge depth and pressure, reservoir pressure, GOR, GOR free; gas: THP, THT, WGR, separator GOR, gas rate, gauge, reservoir pressure), then five actions:

   - **Estimate U Value** - the overall heat transfer coefficient that reproduces each measured tubing head temperature; **Accept** writes the median to the Geothermal gradient section.
   - Correlations are picked PROSPER-style from a list with a checkbox beside each (All / None buttons); after a run the list shows each correlation's mean gauge error (comparison) or matched Parameter 1 and 2 (Match VLP) beside its name.
   - **Correlation Comparison** - TUFFP Unified (the Zhang, Wang, Sarica & Brill 2003 unified mechanistic model via slug dynamics, all inclinations: flow pattern from the slug equations rather than a map - dispersed bubble, bubbly, slug, stratified, annular - with slug holdup, film holdup and slug frequency; implementation choices where the papers leave them open are listed in `wellmodel/unified.py`, among them no droplet entrainment below the Turner/Wallis drop-carrying velocity and a Whalley & Hewitt interfacial friction at high gas density; about 10x slower than the empirical correlations), PE5-style (a calibrated approximation of Petroleum Experts' proprietary PE5 from one PROSPER PE5 gradient: Griffith bubble flow, which PE5 reports as dispersed bubble with a 0.8 ft/s slip; everything else slug flow - PE5 reports slug even at 110 ft/s gas velocity with 2 % liquid (Tutorial 38), so there is no mist region; slug holdup the larger of a 0.32 ft/s slip holdup (low velocity, Abura 2S) and a drift flux vsg/(1-HL) = (1.0421 - 0.3058 lambda) vm + 1.710 lambda^0.633 ft/s (the drift grows with the liquid fraction; capped at 3 x the no-slip holdup), in-situ density gravity and a mixture Moody friction scaled by exp(-2.284 + 0.299 HL + 0.1034 ln vm - 0.724 ln mu_g + 0.329 ln(rho_g/rho_l)) - fitted on 624 nodes of thirteen PROSPER PE5 gradients: Tutorial 38 (condensate, 2-9 % liquid, 19-115 ft/s, BO and EoS, wellhead 500-2000 psig) and Tutorial 47 (water-rich, 3-38 % liquid, 5-53 ft/s, 350 and 1750 psig wellhead): holdup within 2.1 % (T38) and 5.2 % (T47) at every node, pressure drop per traverse within 3.8 % (T47 within 2.2 %), Abura 2S holdup within 0.006 and total gradient within 2 % (the earlier fit on T38 alone was 1 % / 2.4 % on T38 but 4-8 % high on T47's VLP); it reproduces the PE5 - Hagedorn & Brown difference within ~5 psi over 10,900 ft; it is not PE5 itself), PE2-style (a calibrated public-domain approximation of Petroleum Experts' proprietary PE2: Griffith bubble flow as in the modified Hagedorn & Brown; in slug flow the larger of a 0.155 ft/s slip holdup and the Hagedorn & Brown holdup (corrected at high gas velocities to PROSPER's T23 gas-lift traverses), slug friction (1.527 - 0.1205 HL) vsl^-0.0819 times the Hagedorn & Brown friction, stepped up by 1 + 0.4797 (λg - 0.241) where the gas velocity number falls below 1.75 + 1.44 Nlv (fitted on five PROSPER PE2 node exports - T23 at 5 and 20 MMscf/d, T33a QuickLook, Tutorial 11 at 80 % and 5 % water cut; segment friction within 4 %), and a static gradient 1.45 % above the in-situ density; Duns & Ros beyond slug flow - calibrated on one PROSPER PE2 gradient, where it reproduces the PE2 - Hagedorn & Brown difference within ~1 psi over 10,900 ft; it is not PE2 itself), Duns & Ros (original and modified), Ansari, Hagedorn & Brown (mod.), Beggs & Brill, Beggs & Brill (no-slip), Gray, Mukherjee & Brill, Orkiszewski, Aziz-Govier-Fogarasi, Fancher & Brown and the No-slip lower bound, unmatched, from THP to the gauge; ranks them by error and plots pressure and temperature traverses against the measured gauge point. **Gradient results** for each correlation (PROSPER layout): node by node pressure, temperature, gradient, holdup, flow regime (Beggs & Brill map, or Griffith bubble limit + Duns & Ros map), static and friction gradients, cumulative gravity and friction losses to the bottom node, superficial/slip/total velocities, diameter, angle, mass and volume rates, phase densities (mixture = no-slip, flowing = with holdup), viscosities, interfacial tensions and formation volume factors, with restriction inlet/outlet rows; CSV per correlation or an Excel workbook with one sheet per correlation.
   - **Match VLP** - regression of Parameter 1 (gravity) and Parameter 2 (friction) for each correlation over all tests, with standard deviation, before/after error and flags when Parameter 1 is outside 0.9-1.1. Suggests the correlation needing the least correction; you choose the one to carry forward.
   - **VLP/IPR** - matched lift curve at each test's THP and ratios against the IPR from tab 3 (rebuilt at the test reservoir pressure where the model allows), laid out as in PROSPER: calculated and measured liquid/oil/water/gas rates (gas/condensate/water for gas wells), calculated and measured bottom hole pressure (the matched VLP at the measured rate) and % differences; a point table of phase rates with IPR and VLP pressure; and the VLP/IPR plot for the selected test, with an all-tests summary. The rate range follows the tests (it is not spread out to the AOF), so very productive wells still find the operating point. Accepting an Adjust IPR result opens VLP/IPR recalculated with the new IPR.
   - **Adjust IPR** - for a chosen test, solves for the PI (P.I. Entry), the hand-entered skin, or the reservoir pressure that puts the IPR through the test point; **Accept** writes it back to the IPR tab.
   - **Match Surface** - for tests with the optional **Manifold Pressure** (and **Choke Size**, in 64ths, when the bean changed between tests) columns filled: from each manifold pressure the calculation works back through the flowline, fittings and choke to the wellhead, starting at the measured tubing head temperature, and regresses the Sachdeva discharge coefficient and a flowline friction multiplier so the calculated wellhead pressure matches the measured THP. Choose the parameters and the flowline correlation; a parameter the data cannot see is held (friction when the choke is critical in every test, Cd without a choke) and values at their bounds or Cd outside 0.5-1.0 are flagged. Shows the fitted values, RMS error before and after, a test-by-test table with choke regime and losses, and a measured-v-calculated plot. **Accept** sends Cd, the friction multiplier and the correlation to the Calculation tab ("Use matched surface parameters"). The example tests carry manifold pressures generated with Cd 0.85 and friction x 1.2.

   The tubing model runs from the Xmas tree to the deepest equipment (taken as the IPR depth) on the tab 4 equipment: survey inclination, tubing / annulus / tubing + annulus geometry, SSSV and restriction pressure drops, and a heat-loss temperature profile from the geothermal gradient, U value and heat capacities. Fluids come from tab 2: matched black oil, gas correlations with condensate carried as wellstream gas, or the PRP equation of state flashed on a pressure-temperature grid. **Load example tests** generates synthetic tests from the current equipment, PVT and IPR (known answer: P1 1.04, P2 1.12).

6. **Calculation - System analysis (IPR + VLP)** - PROSPER-style nodal analysis at the bottom node. Inputs: top node - the **manifold** (through the surface equipment) or the wellhead - and its pressure, water cut and total GOR (oil) or WGR and separator GOR (gas) - copied from any VLP test with one click - the vertical lift correlation with Parameter 1/2 from Match VLP (or entered by hand), rate method (automatic geometric, automatic linear or user-selected rates), solution node - **bottom node** (default: IPR against VLP at the bottom of the downhole equipment, the solution pressure is the flowing bottom-hole pressure) or **top node** (the inflow - IPR, tubing traversed upwards and, with a manifold top node, the surface equipment downstream - against the top-node pressure; a critical choke shows as a vertical drop at the rate it sets; the solution rate is the same at either node), left-hand intersection (allow/disallow) and stability flags. With the manifold as top node the calculation works back from the manifold pressure through the flowline, fittings (equivalent length) and choke to the wellhead, then down the tubing: choose the surface (flowline) correlation (Beggs & Brill default, Mukherjee & Brill, Beggs & Brill no-slip, Duns & Ros, no-slip), the choke method - Sachdeva et al. (1986, critical and subcritical two-phase flow, discharge coefficient 0.75 by default) or the Gilbert, Ros, Baxendell and Achong critical-flow correlations for oil wells (subcritical points fall back to Sachdeva) - and the fluid cools along each pipe towards the surrounding temperature with the surface U value. Elevation changes come from the TVD column of the surface table and the rate multiplier scales the rate through each item. Results: solution rates (liquid, oil, water, gas or gas, condensate, water), bottom node pressure, wellhead temperature, the IPR/VLP plot, the curve table, and a solution panel with dP friction and gravity in the production string, restriction pressure drops and regime, transit time, maximum/minimum no-slip and erosional velocities (API RP 14E, C = 100), pressure range and stability; with surface equipment also the wellhead pressure, manifold temperature, dP choke, dP flowline and fittings, the choke regime (critical/subcritical) and an item-by-item surface table.

**Multilayer IPR with wellbore dP** (PROSPER *MultiLayer - dP Loss in WellBore*, validated against Tutorial 11): a PROSPER-style layer table - perforated and blank layers with measured and true vertical depth of each layer bottom, layer pressure (at the layer bottom), flowing radius and roughness of the pipe from the node above, and for each perforated layer its own inflow model (oil: PI or Darcy; gas: C and n or Jones) and PVT (oil: GOR, oil gravity, gas gravity, water cut; gas: CGR, WGR, gas gravity); Darcy/Jones thickness is the TVD from the node above. The well is marched up from the deepest layer: each layer adds its inflow at the pressure at its bottom (straight line above its own bubble point, Vogel / Petrobras composite below; Darcy PI from that layer's own Bo and viscosity at its pressure), or takes fluid when the wellbore is above its pressure (crossflow), and the commingled stream - rates, water cut and GOR blended from the layers - loses pressure between nodes by a chosen multiphase correlation; zero roughness turns the losses off, as in PROSPER. Results: the IPR at TOP, each layer's curve on the IPR plot, and a layer-by-layer table (pressure, liquid, oil, water and gas rates). In the system calculation the water cut and GOR (or CGR and WGR) follow the layer contributions at every rate, and the solution lists each layer's contribution. import wellmodel as wm

```python
match = sel.matches[sel.recommended]
model = match.model(base)

op = model.operating_point(pr=3500, whp=250, wc=0.3, gor=600)
mc = wm.monte_carlo(model, {"reservoir_pressure": wm.Triangular(3400, 3500, 3600),
                            "whp": 250, "water_cut": wm.Uniform(0.25, 0.35), "gor": 600},
                    match=match)
```

## Limitations (screening level)

- Oil-well liquid surface tension follows PROSPER's (gas-oil after Abdul-Majeed scaled to PROSPER, gas-water by a refitted Macleod-Sugden, liquid weighted 1 : 0.037 oil : water by surface fractions) - fitted to one oil (Logie, T23 exports, 80 % water cut); the volume-weighted value used before was 3 times PROSPER's at high water cut.
- Multipoint gas lift: a throttling casing-operated valve opens a = (0.00117 - 0.00136 R) (Pc - Pvo) / R of its port - an open flow area growing with the pressure above the opening pressure in proportion to the bellows area (Ab = Ap / R); the constant 0.00108 at R 0.066 (16/64 Camco R-20), 0.00103 at 0.103, 0.00097 at 0.147, 0.00095 at 0.165 (1 in valves). With critical flow through the valve it passes min(a, 1) of its full-port Thornhill-Craver gas. With subcritical flow (Pt / Pc above the critical ratio, d = Pt/Pc - 0.551) PROSPER keeps the valve throttling past a = 1: at a = 1 it passes 1 - d of the full-port gas and opens fully only at a = 1 + 2.5 d (straight lines from a = 0.9 - d). Fitted to the user's PROSPER multipoint VLP runs on the Tutorial 23 well (500 psig, 80 % water cut, 1000-3500 STB/d): Camco R-20 (1.5 in) at 12, 16, 20 and 24/64 with 1360-1510 psig casing, McMurry R-1 and Otis-Merla NM16R (1 in valves) at 16/64, the 16/64 valves at 1460 and 1510 psig, and the A2 casing-pressure sweep (16/64 valves, 2000 STB/d, 1400-1560 psig in 10 psig steps - each valve from throttling to fully open; 270 valve results). Against the sweep: every valve rate within 0.061 MMscf/d (min(a, 1) gave up to 0.24 near full opening), bottom-node pressure within 7 psi, the same statuses but one (valve 3 at 1420 psig: PROSPER just open). Against all the multipoint runs: valve rates rms 0.038 MMscf/d, within 15 % (0.05 MMscf/d) everywhere, bottom-node pressures within 10 psi in most points (up to 40 psi). The largest remaining differences are the 20 and 24/64 valves at low opening (their constant scatters 0.0009-0.0011 with the pressure ratio). The earlier rule (0.247 MMscf/d per psi per in2 of port, fitted to one 24/64 valve) gave a third too little gas at 20/64 and a third of PROSPER's at 12/64, and 2.5 times too much for the 1 in valves. Near the collapse of the injection PROSPER's VLP takes the collapsed state where an unloaded state also exists (A1 2500 / 3000, A2 3500 STB/d), or no injection where a little remains here (C1 / C2 at 3000-3500): this tool, starting from the most gas the valves can pass, finds the unloaded state, so its injection collapses at a somewhat higher rate. PROSPER's multipoint system rates in these runs (2746.8, 3245.7, 2799.5, 2765.9 STB/d) are its VLP rate points interpolated straight across that collapse (reproduced to 0.2 STB/d from its own VLP and IPR values); where its VLP never collapses within the rates (B1-B3, the 24/64 run, A3) it reports no solution. PROSPER's Petex valve coefficients (`gl_valve_dynamics.csv`) are not used: their equations are not published.

- The horizontal-well friction model treats wellbore flow as single phase. The multilayer dP model has no per-layer gravel pack or Karakas & Tariq skin yet (sand control is not applied to it), and its TOP temperature is the reservoir temperature (PROSPER adds a small heating with drawdown). Fractured wells assume infinite-conductivity geometry with a Cinco-Ley correction; bilinear flow is not modelled.
- Steady-state VLP at each forecast step - no slugging or start-up dynamics.
- VLP: acceleration pressure loss is neglected; SSSV/restrictions use a homogeneous orifice equation (Cd 0.9); with the equation of state the wellstream keeps the PRP composition (test GOR/CGR are not used to re-mix it); downhole rate multipliers are not applied yet; the surface equipment is calibrated by Match Surface only from tests with a manifold pressure (the Gilbert-type chokes have no discharge coefficient, so the match uses Sachdeva); the choke is taken as isothermal and Sachdeva uses a fixed gas heat-capacity ratio k = 1.26; restriction regime uses a 0.53 pressure-ratio rule.
- Single-layer homogeneous reservoir; forecast water cut and GOR follow trend functions, not relative permeability.
- **Validated on synthetic data only.** Check against real field tests before relying on results.
